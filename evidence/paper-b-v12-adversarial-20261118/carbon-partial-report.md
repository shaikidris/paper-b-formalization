## 2. Audit of Sections 1–3 and Quantified Hypotheses (Lemmas 2.1–3.2)

### Section 1 (Model, Theorem 1.1, Corollary 1.2)

### Lemma 2.1 (Sibling quotients $H_d$)
- **Quantified hypotheses:** Retained graph contains only vertices reachable from $r$ (discarding unreachable vertices and their outgoing arcs cannot destroy or create $r$-to-$t$ paths in $G-F$, since no $r$-path can visit an unreachable tail). For every retained arc $x \to y$:
  1. $y \ne r$ because $x$ is reachable from $r$ and $G$ is acyclic; hence $d = \operatorname{idom}(y)$ exists.
  2. $x \in D(d)$ because any $r$-to-$x$ path avoiding $d$ would extend via $x \to y$ to $y$ avoiding $d$.
  3. $x \notin D(y)$ because $y$ dominating $x$ gives a path $y \leadsto x$, which with $x \to y$ would form a cycle.
- **Acyclicity and block non-reentry:** Entry from outside any block $D(w)$ (and likewise $D(d)$) can occur only at its root $w$, else a source path would bypass $w$. Consequently, any source path visiting $w$ never leaves and re-enters $D(w)$ (re-entry would have to occur at $w$, violating acyclicity of $G$). In $H_d$, for every quotient arc $u \to y$ (or $d \to y$) from $x \to y$, $u$ (or $d$) dominates $x$, so $\operatorname{top}_G(u) \le \operatorname{top}_G(x) < \operatorname{top}_G(y)$; hence original topological order restricts to a topological order of $H_d$.
- **Single-deletion survival, projection/lifting, and counts:** For distinct children $u \ne y$ of $d$, an $r$-to-$y$ path avoiding $u$ avoids all of $D(u)$, enters $D(d)$ at $d$, stays in $D(d)$, and crosses between blocks only at block roots (each with $\operatorname{idom} = d$), projecting to a $d$-to-$y$ path in $H_d - \{u\}$. Conversely, internal paths $w \leadsto x$ inside each block $D(w)$ lift any chosen sequence of labelled quotient arcs (preserving choices among parallel original tails). Each retained $w \ne r$ appears once as a child in $H_{\operatorname{idom}(w)}$ and at most once as root of $H_w$, plus $r$ at most once, totaling $\le 2n - 2 < 2n$ vertex copies and $\le m$ quotient arcs.

### Lemma 2.2 (Last-exit test)
- **Comparable / single / zero failures:** Handled before Lemma 2.2 by ordinary $\mathcal D$-ancestry; reflexive domination ($t \in D(t)$) marks $t \in F$ as lost.
- **Incomparable failures $a \in D(u), b \in D(v)$ ($B = \{u, v\}$ in $H_d$):**
  - *Outside $D(d)$ or $t = d$:* For $t \notin D(d)$ (vacuous if $d = r$), an $r$-to-$t$ path avoiding $d$ never enters $D(d)$. For $t = d$, $\operatorname{top}_G(d) < \min(\operatorname{top}_G(a), \operatorname{top}_G(b))$, so every $r$-to-$d$ path avoids $\{a, b\}$.
  - *Within $D(u)$ (and symmetrically $D(v)$):* If $a = u$, $D(a) = D(u)$ and $u$ dominates $t$ (lost). If $a \in D(u) \setminus \{u\}$ and $t \in D(u) \setminus D(a)$, an $r$-to-$u$ path avoiding $v$ avoids $D(v) \ni b$ and precedes $a$ topologically ($\operatorname{top}_G(u) < \operatorname{top}_G(a)$), while the suffix $u \leadsto t$ of an $a$-avoiding $r$-to-$t$ path stays inside $D(u)$ by block non-reentry, avoiding $D(v) \ni b$.
  - *Third block $D(y)$ ($y \notin B$):* Reaching $t \in D(y)$ is equivalent to reaching $y$ since $D(y) \cap \{a, b\} = \varnothing$. When $y \in J(B)$, $X^B_u(y) \ne \varnothing$ and $X^B_v(y) \ne \varnothing$ because $H_d - \{v\}$ has a $d$-to-$y$ path (flatness) that must visit $u$, and its last arc out of $u$ witnesses $X^B_u(y)$. Since $X^B_s(y) \ne \varnothing$ is a subset of $D(s)$, every tail in $X^B_s(y)$ lies in $D(a)$ iff $\sigma^B_s(y) = \operatorname{LCA}_{\mathcal D}(X^B_s(y)) \in D(a)$.

### Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2 (Forest of proper losses and $O((n_H+m_H)L^3)$ construction)
- **Quantified hypotheses:** $H$ is a flat rooted DAG ($n_H$ vertices, $m_H$ labelled arcs, root $\rho$), and $B$ ranges over unordered pairs of distinct nonroot vertices.
- **First proper loss and $|V(H)|$ node bound:** Any $y \in J(B)$ must follow both endpoints of $B$ in topological order (before the later endpoint $v$, deleting $v$ cannot affect paths to $y$, and deleting $u$ alone loses only $u$ by flatness). At $y_0 = \min_{\operatorname{top}} J(B)$, no predecessor can be $\rho$ or outside $Z(B)$, and none lies in $J(B)$ by minimality of $y_0$; flatness forbids a single nonroot predecessor, forcing $\operatorname{Pred}_H(y_0) = B$. Thus $B \mapsto \min_{\operatorname{top}} J(B)$ injects $\{B : J(B) \ne \varnothing\}$ into $V(H) \setminus \{\rho\}$, bounding real forest nodes by $n_H - 1 \le |V(H)|$.
- **Prefix property and $m_y$-split:** Earlier edges satisfy $P \subseteq Z(B) \implies Z(P) \subseteq Z(B)$, so along the ancestor path of $c(m_y)$ the condition $\operatorname{Pred}_H(y) \subseteq Z(B)$ is monotone (upward-closed toward the root), justifying binary search on depth. Any pair $B$ properly losing $y$ and omitting $m_y$ properly loses $m_y$, hence lies on $c(m_y)$'s path; any pair $B$ properly losing $y$ and containing $m_y$ cannot properly lose any $p \in \operatorname{Pred}_H(y) \setminus \{m_y\}$ because $\operatorname{top}(p) < \operatorname{top}(m_y)$, forcing $p \in B$ and hence $\operatorname{Pred}_H(y) = \{p, m_y\}$.
- **Test — Reuse of a predecessor-pair key:** Suppose $y_1, y_2$ ($\operatorname{top}(y_1) < \operatorname{top}(y_2)$) both have $\operatorname{Pred}_H(y_1) = \operatorname{Pred}_H(y_2) = P = \{p, m\}$. At $y_1$, $B_*$ is determined solely by $p, m, c(p), c(m)$ and their ancestor paths, which are already frozen because $\operatorname{top}(p) < \operatorname{top}(m) < \operatorname{top}(y_1)$ and later insertions only add leaves. Installing $P$ below $B_*$ at $y_1$ does not alter $c(m)$ (indeed $m \notin J(P)$). At $y_2$, the non-$m$ pairs losing $\{p, m\}$ are still the exact ancestors of $B_*$, and the unique pair containing $m$ losing $\{p, m\}$ is still $P$, whose parent is $B_*$. Reusing the existing node $P$ and setting $c(y_2) = P$ preserves the exact ancestor invariant without duplicating nodes or corrupting parent pointers.
- **Low-bit jump bound and edge-scan charges in Lemma 3.2:** Depth is $\le n_H$. From depth $j$ to $d_{\text{dest}} \le j$, usable jumps $j \mapsto j - \operatorname{lowbit}(j) \ge d_{\text{dest}}$ clear set bits ($\le L$ per run), while an overshoot $j - 2^k < d_{\text{dest}} < j$ ($k \ge 1$) takes one parent step to $j-1$, keeping all subsequent depths strictly between $j - 2^k$ and $j$ where every $\operatorname{lowbit}$ is $\le 2^{k-1}$. This gives $O(L^2)$ moves (and this bound is tight: from $j = 2^k$ to $d_{\text{dest}} = 1$, the traversal visits $2^k \to 2^k - 1 \to \dots \to 2^{k-1} \to 2^{k-1}-1 \to \dots \to 1$ in $\binom{k+1}{2} = \Theta(L^2)$ moves). During the binary search at $y$ ($O(L)$ probe depths), each of the $k_y$ deduplicated predecessors $z$ of $y$ tests $z \in Z(B_{\text{test}})$ by an endpoint check plus one $O(L^2)$ ancestor-at-depth jump from $c(z)$, costing $O(k_y L^3)$ at $y$ and $\sum_y O(k_y L^3) = O((n_H + m_H)L^3)$ overall.

## 3. Bidirectional Reconstruction of Theorem 4.1 and Edge-Case Stress Tests

Let $P$ be a real child of a real pair $B$ in the forest of $H_d$, let $s \in B$, and let $y \in J(P)$.
- **Well-definedness:** By Lemma 3.1, $P \neq B$ (keys are deduplicated), $P \subseteq Z(B)$ so $P \setminus B \subseteq Z(B) \setminus B = J(B)$, and $J(P) \subseteq J(B)$ so $y \in J(B)$. Thus $X^B_s(y)$, $X^B_s(p)$ for all $p \in P \setminus B$, and $X^P_s(y)$ when $s \in P$ are all defined, and $\operatorname{top}(b) < \operatorname{top}(p)$ for all $b \in B$ and $p \in P \setminus B$.

### Inclusion $(\supseteq)$: Right-hand side $\subseteq X^B_s(y)$
1. **Fresh-endpoint contribution ($p \in P \setminus B$):** Let $x \in X^B_s(p)$, witnessed by a quotient arc $s \xrightarrow{x} w$ and a path $Q_1 : w \leadsto p$ in $H_d - B$. Write $P = \{p, q\}$. Since $y \in J(P)$, $y \notin P$; by Lemma 2.1 (flatness), $H_d - \{q\}$ contains a path $R : d \leadsto y$. Because $y \in Z(P)$, $R$ cannot avoid $p$, so $R$ has a suffix $Q_2 : p \leadsto y$ in $H_d$. Every vertex $z$ on $Q_2$ satisfies $\operatorname{top}(z) \ge \operatorname{top}(p) > \max_{b \in B} \operatorname{top}(b)$, so $Q_2$ avoids $B$. Concatenating $Q_1$ and $Q_2$ in the DAG $H_d - B$ yields a path $w \leadsto y$ in $H_d - B$ from the same labelled arc $s \xrightarrow{x} w$, placing $x \in X^B_s(y)$.
2. **Inherited contribution ($s \in B \cap P$):** Here $|B \cap P| = 1$; write $B = \{s, v\}$ and $P = \{s, p\}$ with $p \in P \setminus B$, so $\operatorname{top}(v) < \operatorname{top}(p)$. Let $x \in X^P_s(y)$, witnessed by $s \xrightarrow{x} w$ and $Q : w \leadsto y$ in $H_d - \{s, p\}$. Suppose for contradiction that $Q$ visits $v$, with suffix $Q[v \leadsto y]$ in $H_d - \{s, p\}$. By flatness, $H_d - \{s\}$ contains a path $R : d \leadsto v$. Every vertex $z$ on $R$ has $\operatorname{top}(z) \le \operatorname{top}(v) < \operatorname{top}(p)$, so $R$ avoids $p$ as well as $s$. Then $R$ followed by $Q[v \leadsto y]$ reaches $y$ from $d$ in $H_d - P$, contradicting $y \in J(P)$. Hence $Q$ avoids $v$, so $Q$ lies in $H_d - \{s, v\} = H_d - B$ and $x \in X^B_s(y)$. (If $s \notin P$, $\varnothing \subseteq X^B_s(y)$ holds trivially.)

### Inclusion $(\subseteq)$: $X^B_s(y) \subseteq$ Right-hand side
Let $x \in X^B_s(y)$, witnessed by $s \xrightarrow{x} w$ and $Q : w \leadsto y$ in $H_d - B$.
1. **$Q$ visits some fresh endpoint $p \in P \setminus B$:** The prefix $Q[w \leadsto p]$ (length $0$ if $w = p$, valid since $p \notin B$) lies in $H_d - B$, so $s \xrightarrow{x} w$ and $Q[w \leadsto p]$ place $x \in X^B_s(p)$.
2. **$Q$ avoids all of $P \setminus B$:** Since $Q$ already avoids $B$, $Q$ avoids $B \cup P \supseteq P$, so $Q$ is a path in $H_d - P$ (in particular $w \notin P$).
   - *Shared endpoint ($s \in P$):* $s \xrightarrow{x} w$ and $Q : w \leadsto y$ in $H_d - P$ directly witness $x \in X^P_s(y)$.
   - *Unshared parent endpoint ($s \notin P$):* Then $P \cap B \subseteq B \setminus \{s\}$ has size $0$ or $1$. By flatness of $H_d$, there exists a path $R : d \leadsto s$ avoiding the at most one vertex in $P \cap B$. Every fresh endpoint $p \in P \setminus B$ satisfies $\operatorname{top}(s) < \operatorname{top}(p)$, so $R$ avoids $P \setminus B$ as well, hence $R$ lies in $H_d - P$. Since $s \notin P$ and $w \notin P$, concatenating $R : d \leadsto s$, $s \to w$, and $Q : w \leadsto y$ reaches $y$ in $H_d - P$, contradicting $y \in J(P)$. Thus this subcase is impossible.

### Explicit Stress Tests

## 4. Audit of Section 5, Theorem 1.1, and Section 6 (Compression, Non-Circularity, Space, Time)

- **LCA commutation and constant-size map closure:** On $(V(\mathcal D) \cup \{\bot\}, \sqcup)$, LCA is an associative, commutative, idempotent semilattice join with identity $\bot$, so $\bigsqcup(A \cup C) = (\bigsqcup A) \sqcup (\bigsqcup C)$ commutes exactly with the union in Theorem 4.1. Each map coordinate has the form $x \mapsto c_i$ or $x \mapsto c_i \sqcup x_{\text{slot}[i]}$ ($\text{slot}[i] \in \{0, 1\}$); composing two maps takes at most two $\sqcup$-operations ($O(L)$ time) and stores two $\mathcal D$-vertices plus two slot tags ($O(1)$ words).
- **Strict non-circularity of initialization in Lemma 5.1:**
  1. When processing $y$ in topological order of $H_d$ with real $c(y)$, at most one pair can first appear at $y$, namely $P = c(y)$ (by Lemma 3.1).
  2. When $P$ first appears at $y$, its map to $B = \operatorname{parent}(P)$ is installed *before* $\text{base}[y]$. If $B = 0$, the map is $(\bot, \bot)$. If $B$ is real, by Lemma 3.1 $P = \operatorname{Pred}_{H_d}(y)$, so every fresh endpoint $p \in P \setminus B$ is a predecessor of $y$ ($\operatorname{top}(p) < \operatorname{top}(y)$) and satisfies $p \in J(B)$, meaning $c(p)$ is real and $\text{base}[p]$ is already computed. Moreover, every node $Q$ on the forest path from $c(p)$ up to $B$ (and from $B$ up to $\operatorname{jump}(P)$, whenever $\operatorname{depth}(\operatorname{jump}(P)) \ge 1$) is an ancestor of $c(p)$ or $B$, hence properly loses $p$, so $\min_{\operatorname{top}} J(Q) \le \operatorname{top}(p) < \operatorname{top}(y)$—thus $Q$'s parent and jump maps were already installed strictly before $y$. (If $\operatorname{depth}(\operatorname{jump}(P)) = 0$, i.e., $\operatorname{depth}(P)$ is a power of $2$, the root map is $(\bot, \bot)$ and no evaluation to any real pair $B$ at depth $\ge 1$ ever takes a jump landing at depth $0$.)
  3. Next, for $C = c(y)$ real, $\text{base}[y]$ starts at $(\bot, \bot)$ and scans each entering arc $z \to y$. Direct arcs $z = s \in C$ join their original tail into coordinate $s$ immediately. For $z \notin C$, $z \in J(C)$ (else a $C$-avoiding path to $z$ extends to $y$), $\operatorname{top}(z) < \operatorname{top}(y)$, and every node on the path from $c(z)$ up to $C$ properly loses $z$, so $\text{base}[z]$ and all maps from $c(z)$ to $C$ were already computed at or before $z$. (Indeed, when $C$ first appears at $y$, $\operatorname{Pred}_{H_d}(y) = C$, so $z \notin C$ never even occurs at first appearance.) Partitioning witnessing paths in $H_d - C$ by their last arc $z \to y$ proves $\text{base}[y] = (\sigma^C_{s_0}(y), \sigma^C_{s_1}(y))$.
- **Heavy-light LCA, preprocessing, peak words, retained words, and online query on the same index:**
  - Heavy-light LCA lifts the **deeper path head** (avoiding the classic bug of lifting the deeper vertex's path head), giving $O(L)$ time per $\sqcup$ with $O(n)$ shared words.
  - Each forest evaluation uses $O(L^2)$ moves $\times$ $O(1)$ $\sqcup$-joins $\times$ $O(L)$ time $= O(L^3)$. Lemma 5.1 performs $\le 1$ traversal per quotient arc ($m_H$ total) and $\le 3$ per real pair ($\le n_H$ total), giving $O((n_H + m_H)L^3)$ time and $O(n_H + m_H)$ working words; summing via $\sum_d n_{H_d} \le 2n$ and $\sum_d m_{H_d} \le m$ yields $O((n+m)L^3)$ total preprocessing time and $O(n+m)$ peak working words.
  - Because each retained vertex $w \ne r$ is a nonroot vertex in a unique quotient $H_{\operatorname{idom}(w)}$, retaining original-to-retained IDs, topological numbers (see Defect 1 below), $\mathcal D$ intervals/children/HLD data, per-vertex `c[w]` and `base[w]`, sorted pair keys per $d$, forest intervals/depths/parents/jumps, and two $O(1)$-word maps per pair node occupies $O(n)$ words total and answers any online query $(F, t)$ in $O(L^3)$ time without adjacency lists or arc labels.
