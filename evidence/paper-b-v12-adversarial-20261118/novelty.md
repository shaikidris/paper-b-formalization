## How closely the 2006 question approaches the oracle

There is already an overlap on a **singleton** supplied set. Reverse the older orientation. Remove endpoint and initially unreachable cases and use ordinary domination to answer whenever either failure alone disconnects \(t\). On the remaining triples of distinct vertices \(a,b,t\), the target is lost to \(\{a,b\}\) exactly when \(\{a,b\}\) is an inclusion-minimal size-two separator for the singleton \(\{t\}\). Indeed, if the pair disconnects \(t\), both single deletions still have a surviving path by hypothesis, so each member is necessary. The converse is the definition of separation. This comparison uses the disjoint-terminal convention in the 2005 Definition 1 and requires no equality test on whole chains.

Section 4 (pp. 36–37) supplies the per-chain side, position and matching-interval labels needed for such a membership test. Section 3 credits the 2005 algorithm with quadratic construction time for one chain and gives linear space for that chain. Section 5 (p. 37) starts the common-set computation from the chains of the individual supplied vertices. Section 6 explicitly lists the circuit, the two vertices and their chains as its input (p. 38). Figure 3 and the accompanying text give two binary searches (p. 39), followed by the two references and prefix sizes (p. 40). Thus the missing bound is the cost of preparing and retaining the required information jointly for every later target. A later linear-time one-target algorithm also leaves that aggregate cost to be accounted for. None of these observations gives a lower bound on chain compression.

The earlier structural operation matches positions in two prepared chains. Theorem 4.1 instead gives an equality of physical exit-tail sets across adjacent separating pairs in a labelled quotient. We found no such equality or constructor in the eight pages. This is a comparison with a specific primary source. Global priority is a different question.

## Check on the general common-set definition

The paper allows different supplied vertices to witness the necessity of the two cut endpoints in Definition 1 (p. 35). This has a consequence for its unqualified multiple-vertex claim. Take two paths in its orientation, \(u_1\to a\to c\to r\) and \(u_2\to b\to d\to r\), with no other arcs. The pair \(\{a,b\}\) is disjoint from \(\{u_1,u_2\}\), intersects every supplied-vertex-to-root path and has a separate witness for each of its two elements. Both individual chains are empty because a vertex with just one path has no irredundant pair. The empty-chain branch of Figure 3 (p. 39) returns empty, although the common pair exists. Its endpoints are also absent from both chains, contradicting the unrestricted necessary condition in Theorem 1 (p. 38). In §7 the authors benchmark triples whose immediate dominator is the root. These two vertices do not satisfy that experimental restriction. The example concerns the general common-set formulation, and leaves the single-target membership comparison intact.

The immediate-dominator restriction explains why this particular defect disappears on the tested triples. Suppose each supplied vertex has the output as its immediate dominator. For a disjoint pair of nonoutput vertices cutting all supplied paths, fix one supplied vertex. Neither cut member alone dominates it, so it has a path avoiding each member separately. Each such path must meet the other cut member. Thus the same pair is irredundant for this individual vertex, and this holds for every supplied vertex. Conversely, a pair common to all the individual chains satisfies the collective definition. This equivalence establishes the appropriate intersection predicate on that restricted domain. The complexity of preparing all its chains is still a separate issue.

The preprocessing distinction suffices for Paper B's comparison even granting the 2006 merger on its intended inputs. We recommend keeping the counterexample in this review unless the manuscript undertakes a direct analysis of the earlier theorem. [Executable check](check_2006_example.py)

## Recommended related-work wording

> Teslenko, Dubrova and Tenhunen report \(O(k\log n)\) time to combine the prepared dominator chains of \(k\) supplied circuit vertices [TDT06, §§5–6]. Here one index is built from the input DAG in \(O((n+m)L^3)\) time and retains \(O(n)\) words for any later target and failure pair.

This proposed wording would replace the two related-work sentences about the 2006 comparison. It describes the earlier algorithm's inputs and credits its distinct operation. The more detailed numerical comparison with Choudhary is already correctly stated.
