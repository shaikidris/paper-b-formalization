Given a directed acyclic graph and a fixed source, we construct a linear-space index that decides whether a target remains reachable after the deletion of two vertices. Both failures and the target may be chosen after preprocessing. For n vertices and m arcs, the deterministic construction takes O((n+m) log³(n+2)) time and O(n+m) working words. The index retains O(n) words and answers a query in O(log³(n+2)) time. The construction groups vertices by their ordinary dominators. Inside each group, the pairs that disconnect other vertices are the nodes of a forest ordered by containment of the sets they disconnect beyond their endpoints. Our exit-transfer theorem expresses the exit tails at a parent cut as the union of a target-independent contribution from the child’s new endpoints and a contribution inherited at a shared endpoint. For a fixed cut and target below it, each endpoint’s exit tails are summarized by their lowest common ancestor in the original dominator tree. For an explicitly listed batch with a separate set of targets for each failure pair, the total time is near-linear in the graph and the lists, including preprocessing.
