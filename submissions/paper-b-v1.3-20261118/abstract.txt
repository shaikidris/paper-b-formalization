Given a directed acyclic graph and a fixed source, we construct a linear-space index that decides whether a target remains reachable after the deletion of up to two vertices. The failures and target may be chosen after preprocessing. For n vertices and m arcs, the deterministic construction takes O((n+m)log²(n+2)) time and O(n+m) working words. The index retains O(n) words and answers a query in O(log(n+2)) time. The construction groups vertices by their ordinary dominators. Inside each group, the pairs that disconnect other vertices are the nodes of a forest ordered by containment of the sets they disconnect beyond their endpoints. Our exit-transfer theorem expresses the exit tails at a parent cut as the union of a target-independent contribution from the child’s new endpoints and a contribution inherited at a shared endpoint. For each endpoint, the smallest and largest preorder ranks of its exit tails in the dominator tree suffice to test whether any tail survives. For an explicitly listed batch with a separate set of targets for each failure pair, the total time is near-linear in the graph and the lists, including preprocessing.
